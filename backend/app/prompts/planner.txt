Task: Transform the topic, thesis, research findings, and verified claims into a cohesive, topic-driven story architecture and creative brief.

CORE PRINCIPLE:
Do not force every video into the same cookie-cutter template. Tailor the narrative architecture directly to the subject matter. Choose an appropriate narrative form:
- Scientific Explanation (for science, nature, physics, or biology)
- Historical Reconstruction (for history, archaeology, or origins)
- Engineering / System Analysis (for complex machines, aerospace, computing, infrastructure)
- Investigative Storytelling / Case Study (for business, economics, crimes, or corporate sagas)
- Problem-Solving / Chronological Journey (for discoveries, dilemmas, or biographical events)

REQUIREMENTS:
1. Formulate a Topic-Specific Creative Brief:
   - "core_premise": Crisp statement of the underlying reality or paradox.
   - "central_question": The driving inquiry the viewer needs answered.
   - "narrative_form": One of the tailored forms above.
   - "chosen_angle": The specific lens that makes this subject immediately compelling.
   - "key_insights": 3-5 concrete discoveries, milestones, or mechanisms grounded in the verified claims.
   - "target_audience_takeaway": What the audience understands by the end.
   - "what_makes_it_interesting": The counterintuitive, surprising, or high-stakes element.
   - "cliches_to_avoid": Generic buzzwords, vague summaries, or irrelevant broad categories.

2. Structure Story Acts:
   - Each act must advance the central question with real evidence from the research dossier.
   - Link each act to specific claim_ids.
   - Define a substantive question, causal twist, and open loop for each act.
   - Allocate word budgets proportional to complexity (total: target_minutes * 140-150 words).

3. Pacing & Retention:
   - The hook must be an immediate immersion into the specific topic—NOT a canned generic metaphor.
   - The payoff must resolve or synthesize the central question.
